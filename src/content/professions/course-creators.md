answerBlock: >-
  A course creator can go from idea to published book in 6 to 12 months by working the process in this

identityMirror: >-
  You've built modules, worksheets, and a launch calendar tighter than most publishing houses manage, and
  you watch students buy in during week one, finish lesson three, and disappear before lesson four. A book
  doesn't ask for that same commitment up front. It's the thing that gets a stranger curious enough to
  enroll in the first place.
obstacles:
  - objection: "My course already covers this; a book would cannibalize it."
    reframe: >-
      A book and a course solve different problems for different readers. Someone who isn't ready to buy
      a course yet will still read a book, and for that reader, the book often becomes the best sales
      page your course will ever have.
  - objection: "Courses are interactive; a book is flat."
    reframe: >-
      True, and that's the point. A book meets the reader who wants to think something through alone
      before they commit to anything interactive. It's a different room for a different moment in their
      decision, not a rerun of the same room.
  - objection: "I'd have to rewrite everything."
    reframe: >-
      You wouldn't. Structured sessions extract the teaching already in your head, or already sitting in
      your modules, in your own words. Editing turns that transcript into chapters. Nobody hands you a
      blank page and asks you to start over.
whatItDoes: >-
  A book that outperforms a lead magnet at the top of your funnel, authority that doesn't depend on a
  platform's algorithm, invitations to speak or guest on podcasts, and students who enroll already sold on
  how you teach. The book is rarely the income. It's what gets someone to trust the course enough to buy
  it.

    answer: >-

  - question: "Does a book compete with my course or feed it?"
    answer: >-
      It feeds it. A book reaches readers who aren't ready to buy yet and warms them up for the day they
      are. Nothing in the book replaces the depth, accountability, or community your course provides; it
      just gets more of the right people to your door.
  - question: "What happens to the book if I update my course later?"
    answer: >-
      Nothing forces you to update the book every time the course changes. A well-positioned book teaches
      principles and a transformation, not a click-by-click tour of your current curriculum, so it keeps
      working even as the course evolves underneath it.
