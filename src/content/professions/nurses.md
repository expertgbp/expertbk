obstacles:
  - objection: "I work twelve-hour shifts. I don't have a spare hour, let alone a spare year."
    reframe: >-
      Correct, and the nurses on our roster didn't have spare hours either. That's why the manuscript is
      extracted in short structured sessions around your schedule, not squeezed out of sleep you don't
      have. One of our authors, a nurse with 53 years at the bedside, arrived with decades of knowledge and
      no way in; the structure was the cure.
  - objection: "I'm not a writer, I'm a nurse."
    reframe: >-
      Most of our authors said the same thing on their first call. You already explain complicated things
      simply, every shift. That's the actual skill a book needs. We supply the structure; you supply what
      you already know.
  - objection: "Can I write about patients and cases I've seen?"
    reframe: >-
      Yes, the same way you'd present a case at an in-service: details changed or combined so the lesson
      holds and no one is identifiable. This gets handled explicitly in editing, not left to guesswork.
  - objection: "Will an editor flatten the way I actually talk?"
    reframe: >-
      Your voice survives editing here. We edit with you, not over you, and the phrases your patients and
      students remember stay yours on the page.
