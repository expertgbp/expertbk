obstacles:
  - objection: "Tax law changes every year; my book will be out of date in twelve months."
    reframe: >-
      True, and also not the problem you think it is. The books that hold up aren't the ones that chase this
      year's rule changes. They teach the thinking behind good decisions: how to read a statement, how to
      plan ahead, how to avoid the mistakes you see every season. Positioning decides what's timeless and
      what's a footnote you can update later.
  - objection: "Compliance rules limit what I can say."
    reframe: >-
      They limit what you can say carelessly, not what you can say at all. Editing is where disclaimers,
      scope language, and anything that reads as personalized advice get handled by design. You review every
      word before it ships. Nothing goes out that you wouldn't stand behind in front of a client.
  - objection: "Nobody wants to read about accounting."
    reframe: >-
      Nobody wants to read about accounting. People want to stop being afraid of an audit, understand why
      they owe what they owe, or finally feel in control of their business finances. The book isn't about
      debits and credits. It's about the fear you already know how to calm in a client meeting.
