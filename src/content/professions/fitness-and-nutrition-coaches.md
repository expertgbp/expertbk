obstacles:
  - objection: "Everything I know is already on YouTube for free."
    reframe: >-
      Information is free. Application is not. Your clients don't need more information, they need your
      voice, your sequencing, and your accountability in a form they can carry into the gym or the
      kitchen. The positioning stage decides what makes your approach different from the video they
      scrolled past yesterday.
  - objection: "I'm not a scientist; who am I to write a book?"
    reframe: >-
      You're not writing a textbook. You're writing down the program that already works, the one you've
      run with real clients in real bodies. That lived coaching experience is exactly what a scientist
      without client hours can't put on the page.
  - objection: "My clients need me in the room, not on a page."
    reframe: >-
      A book doesn't replace you, it extends you. It's the version of you a prospect meets before they
      ever book a session, and the version an existing client re-reads on the days you're not there to
      say it out loud.

  podcast invitations, a name coaches recognize outside your gym or your zip code, and the credibility to
  launch a group program or course your book naturally leads into.

    answer: >-

  - question: "Can I make health claims in my book?"
    answer: >-
      You can share what worked for you and your clients, framed as your experience and approach rather
      than a guarantee. Editing flags language that oversteps into medical or diagnostic claims so the
      book stays honest and defensible.
  - question: "What if I coach a narrow niche, like postpartum athletes or masters lifters?"
    answer: >-
      Narrow is often the advantage. A precise book for one kind of client outperforms a vague book for
      everyone. Positioning decides this before a word is written.
