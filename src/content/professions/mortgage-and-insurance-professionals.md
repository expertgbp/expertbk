obstacles:
  - objection: "My industry is heavily regulated; what can I even say?"
    reframe: >-
      More than you think, once it's framed correctly. The book teaches how to think about a decision, not
      how to structure a specific product for a specific reader. Editing handles disclaimers and scope
      language by design, and you review every word before it ships. Nothing goes out that your compliance
      team would flag.
  - objection: "Clients see me as a salesperson, not an expert."
    reframe: >-
      A book changes that math before the first meeting. Someone who reads your explanation of how rates
      actually work, or how to choose the right coverage, walks in already seeing you as the person who
      taught them something, not the person who's trying to sell them something.
  - objection: "Rates and products change; the book will age fast."
    reframe: >-
      Rates change. The fear of getting a six-figure decision wrong doesn't. Positioning decides what's a
      timeless principle worth putting in the book and what's a number that belongs in a blog post you
      update instead. That's what keeps the book useful past this quarter's rate.

  drawer, speaking invitations, media quotes, and a door to workshops and courses that a license alone can't
  open. The book is rarely the income. It's the instrument that brings the client to you before they ever
  call around for quotes.
