answerBlock: >-
  A first responder or veteran can move from idea to published book in 6 to 12 months by working the
  process in this order: position the book around who it's really for, extract the manuscript through

  without your review.
identityMirror: >-
  The shift never fully ends, not really, even years after the badge or the uniform comes off. There are
  stories you only tell people who were there, and lessons that could shorten someone else's hardest year
  if they ever reached them. A book is one way those lessons keep working long after you've stopped.
obstacles:
  - objection: "Some of what I know, I'm not sure I should write down."
    reframe: >-
      That instinct deserves respect, not an argument. Positioning and editing exist to help you decide,
      page by page, what serves a reader and what stays yours. Nothing goes in the book without your
      review, and nothing has to go in at all.
  - objection: "I'm not a writer; I'm the person who shows up."
    reframe: >-
      You don't have to be. The manuscript is extracted through structured conversation, not a blank
      page. You talk, in your own words, about what you know, and that becomes the material the book is
      built from.
  - objection: "Civilians won't understand, and my own people will judge."
    reframe: >-
      Both fears are reasonable, and you're not the first person on our roster to carry them. Positioning
      starts by asking who the book is actually for, often the person a few years behind you rather than
      the general public, and editing keeps the language honest to the people who were there.
whatItDoes: >-
  A book like this can become training material for the person coming up behind you, a record your family
  gets to keep, and proof that hard experience can turn into something useful for someone else. The rights
  stay 100% yours, the same as every other path here.

      anything, at any time.
  - question: "Do I have to include the hardest parts?"
    answer: >-
      No. What goes in the book is entirely your call. Positioning helps you decide what serves a reader,
      and anything you'd rather keep for yourself simply stays yours.
