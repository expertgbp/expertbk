obstacles:
  - objection: "Is it too late to start? I'm not building a career anymore."
    reframe: >-
      One of our authors first thought about her book in high school and started at 76, decades later. It's
      never too late to put thirty years of judgment on paper; the only real deadline is the one you set for
      yourself, like a birthday, an anniversary, or a grandchild old enough to read it.
  - objection: "I have thirty years of decisions. Where would I even start?"
    reframe: >-
      The most common feeling among executives at this stage, and structure is the answer, not more
      thinking. Structured sessions pull out the decisions that matter most and organize them, so you're
      talking, not staring at a blank page.
  - objection: "I don't know if anyone besides my family would want to read this."
    reframe: >-
      Positioning decides that early: is this a book for family, for your industry, or both. Either answer
      is valid, and it changes how the book gets built, not whether it gets written.
  - objection: "Will an editor turn my story into something that doesn't sound like me?"
    reframe: >-
      Your voice survives editing here. We edit with you, not over you, and the way you actually tell a
      story at the dinner table stays on the page.
