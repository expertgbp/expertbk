**Short answer:** a vanity press takes your money and prints whatever you send, with no editing, no positioning, and no say in whether the book is actually ready. A real hybrid (partnership) publisher earns its fee by doing the work: positioning the book before a word is drafted, editing with you instead of over you, and running a real launch. The seven differences below are the checklist to run before you sign anything, from anyone, including us.

## Do they edit the manuscript, or just print it?

A vanity press typically runs a light proofread, if that, and sends the file to print. A real partnership publisher runs structural and line edits, because a manuscript that hasn't been shaped by someone other than its author rarely reads the way its author thinks it does.

Ask to see a sample edit before you commit. If the answer is vague, that's the answer.

## Do you keep your rights and royalties?

This is non-negotiable on our side, and it should be non-negotiable on yours. You should keep 100% of your rights and royalties in a partnership model. Some vanity presses bury rights language in a contract you're expected not to read closely. Read it closely anyway.

## Is there a real positioning stage before writing starts?

Positioning means deciding the one reader, the one promise, and the one shelf your book competes on, before a single chapter gets drafted. A vanity press skips this, because it doesn't affect their fee either way. A partnership publisher treats it as the most important stage in the whole process, because an unpositioned book is the single biggest reason books sell fewer than 100 copies.

## Do they help you launch, or do they stop at "in print"?

Printing a book and launching a book are two different jobs. A vanity press's job usually ends the day the file goes live on a retailer. A partnership publisher builds a launch plan: reviews, a release sequence, and a plan for the weeks after the book goes live, because a book isn't an event. It's an asset that needs a push.

## Are the founders verifiable?

Look up who's actually running the company. Can you find them, their credentials, and their own track record? Our founders, Susmita Dutta and Ash Goel, are USA Today and Wall Street Journal bestselling authors and TEDx speakers; that's checkable, not a claim you have to take on faith. A publisher that can't be found is a publisher you can't verify.

## Is the price itemized, or one vague bundle?

A partnership publisher should be able to tell you exactly what you're paying for: writing support, editing passes, design, distribution, and launch, each priced on its own. A vanity press often quotes one bundled number designed to be hard to compare against anything else. Vagueness is the tell.
