The old launch plan was built around one goal: get mentioned somewhere big. That's no longer where the real return is, and 2026 book publicity coverage backs up what we've been telling authors for a while.

## What the trend actually says

Industry coverage heading into 2026 points to hyper-targeted podcasts, niche trade publications, and vertical newsletters becoming [some of the most valuable placements](https://www.smithpublicity.com/2025/12/7-book-publicity-trends-for-2026-what-is-changing-new-strategies-for-authors-and-experts-to-reach-the-right-media/) for nonfiction authors, especially in business, leadership, health, and workplace topics. The term some outlets are using is "Micro-Authority Media," and it's expected to keep growing through 2026.

## Why a smaller, exact audience beats a bigger, general one

A general-interest media mention reaches a lot of people who will never buy a business book on your specific subject. A niche podcast episode reaches fewer people, but nearly all of them are already the reader you wrote the book for. For someone using the book to generate speaking invitations or client conversations, that second number is the one that actually moves the needle.

## What this looks like in practice

Instead of a press list of national outlets, a 2026 launch plan for one of our authors looks more like: five to ten podcasts their exact reader already subscribes to, one or two trade newsletters in their industry, and a handful of niche communities (a Facebook group, a Slack community, a book club) where the topic is already being discussed. It's slower to build than blasting a press release, but it converts at a completely different rate.
