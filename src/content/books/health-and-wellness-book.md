painPoints:
  - objection: "I know too much. I'll overwhelm a reader who just wants one answer."
    reframe: >-
      This is the most common pain we see from clinicians and health professionals. Your reader may be
      scared and looking for one specific answer, not a textbook. The positioning stage decides what goes in
      the book, what becomes a course, and what stays in the clinic.
  - objection: "Will this get me in trouble with my license, my board, or a patient?"
    reframe: >-
      Patient and client privacy, claims discipline, and scope-of-practice language are handled in editing
      by design. You review every word. Nothing ships that you wouldn't defend in front of a colleague.
  - objection: "Health advice changes constantly. What if the book is outdated in two years?"
    reframe: >-
      Positioning separates timeless principles from specific protocols likely to shift. The book is built
      to age well on the parts that matter most, and updates are possible at future print runs.
  - objection: "I don't have time to write between patients, clients, or shifts."
    reframe: >-
      Correct, and the clinicians on our roster didn't either. The manuscript is extracted, not squeezed
      out, in structured sessions that fit around clinic or client hours, your talking, our system.
processDifferences: >-
  A health and wellness book carries a compliance layer a standard nonfiction extraction does not: claims
  language, scope-of-practice boundaries, and privacy handling get reviewed explicitly during editing, not
  left to chance. Extraction sessions are also built to translate clinical language into plain language a
  frightened or overwhelmed reader can act on immediately, since the reader here is often in crisis, not
  browsing casually.

faqs:
  - question: "Can I write about real patients or clients?"
    answer: >-
      Yes, with the same care you already apply in practice. Details get abstracted or composited so the
      lesson stays intact and no one is identifiable. This is handled explicitly in editing.
  - question: "What if my specialty feels too narrow for a whole book?"
    answer: >-
      Narrow is often the advantage. A precise book for one kind of patient or one specific condition
      outperforms a vague book for everyone, and positioning decides this before a word is written.
  - question: "Will an editor water down my clinical language?"
    answer: >-
      No. We edit with you, not over you. Clinical precision stays intact where it matters; language gets
      simplified where a scared reader needs plain words, not where accuracy would suffer.
  - question: "How long does a health and wellness book take to write and publish?"
    answer: >-
      Most physician, nurse, and health coach authors go from idea to published book in 6 to 12 months,
      working structured sessions around clinic or client hours.
  - question: "Do I keep the rights to what I write?"
    answer: >-
      Yes. 100% of your rights and royalties, always. That's the difference between partnership publishing
      and a traditional deal.
