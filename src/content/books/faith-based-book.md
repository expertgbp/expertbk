answerBlock: >-
  A faith-based book gets written as an act of stewardship, not a race. The process starts by naming what
  this book is for and who it's meant to reach, then moves through extraction sessions paced to your own
  sense of timing. Editing, publishing, and launch follow with no manufactured deadlines or countdowns.
  You keep 100% of your rights and royalties, and the book moves only as fast as the assignment itself
  feels ready to move.
whoWritesThis: >-
  Faith-driven authors write these, often after years, sometimes decades, of feeling called to write this
  specific book. You may already use words like "steward" and "assignment" for this. We use them too,
  because that's genuinely what this is: not a marketing project, but something you've been asked to
  finish.
painPoints:
  - objection: "I've felt called to write this for years. Why has it taken so long?"
    reframe: >-
      That gap is common, not a sign you've failed the assignment. Timing is part of stewardship. What
      matters now is that you're ready to move, and the process is built to honor that timing rather than
      override it with someone else's deadline.
  - objection: "Who am I to write this? I'm not a pastor or a theologian."
    reframe: >-
      Most faith-based authors on our roster aren't either. The assignment usually belongs to someone who
      lived it, not someone who studied it from a pulpit. Your specific story is the credential.
  - objection: "I don't want this to feel rushed or sold like a product."
    reframe: >-
      It won't be. There is no manufactured urgency here, no countdown built to pressure a decision. The
      pace is set by what feels right for the book and for you, and the process is built around that on
      purpose.
  - objection: "I want to be faithful to the message, not just make it a bestseller."
    reframe: >-
      That priority is respected, not overridden. The positioning stage asks what this book is for and who
      it's meant to reach, and that answer, not a sales target, shapes every decision after it.
processDifferences: >-
  A faith-based book is never paced by a cart-style countdown or a limited-time offer; urgency of that kind
  has no place here. Extraction sessions open by naming the book's purpose and intended reader in
  stewardship terms, the assignment, the timing, rather than a market positioning pitch. Language choices
  during editing mirror the author's own faith vocabulary respectfully, and the schedule flexes around
  spiritual readiness rather than a sales calendar.
