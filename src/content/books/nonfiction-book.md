painPoints:
  - objection: "I have too much knowledge, not too little. Where do I even start?"
    reframe: >-
      That's the most common version of this problem we see, and it's a positioning problem, not a
      knowledge problem. The Engineer stage decides what goes in this book, what becomes a second book or a
      course, and what stays a conversation you have one-on-one.
  - objection: "I don't know if what I know is actually book material."
    reframe: >-
      If you've answered the same question for clients, patients, or students for years, it's book
      material. The proof is that people keep asking you the same thing and keep being surprised by the
      answer.
  - objection: "How is a book different from just a really long blog post?"
    reframe: >-
      A blog post answers one question. A book builds an argument across chapters, with a beginning that
      earns an ending. Structure is what separates a stack of good ideas from something a reader finishes
      and recommends.
  - objection: "I've started this book three times and stalled every time."
    reframe: >-
      Most stalls happen because the writer is doing structure and prose at the same time, alone, without a
      deadline. Structured extraction separates those two jobs: you talk through your material in guided
      sessions, and the architecture gets built around what you say.
