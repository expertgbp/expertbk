painPoints:
  - objection: "There are already a thousand books like mine. Why would anyone pick this one?"
    reframe: >-
      There probably are a thousand books that sound like yours from a distance. Almost none of them are
      written for one specific reader with one specific promise. Positioning happens before extraction starts,
      and it's the difference between "a book about leadership" and "the book a first-time VP reads the week
      she gets promoted."
  - objection: "My ideas are scattered across decks, keynotes, and client emails. I don't know how to make that a book."
    reframe: >-
      That scattered material is an asset, not a mess. It means the thinking is already tested on real
      rooms and real clients. Structured extraction sessions pull it into one spine; you talk through your
      framework, we build the architecture around it.
  - objection: "I don't want to sound like every other LinkedIn guru with a book."
    reframe: >-
      Your voice, your coined terms, and your specific stories are the IP that keeps you from sounding like
      everyone else. We edit with you, not over you, so the phrases you've been saying in board rooms for
      years stay yours on the page.
  - objection: "I have a business to run. I can't take a year off to write."
    reframe: >-
      You won't. The manuscript is extracted in structured sessions that fit around a real calendar, not
      squeezed out in evenings you don't have. The heavy lifting after that sits with the team, not you.
processDifferences: >-
  Every nonfiction book goes through structured extraction, editing, and launch. A business book front-loads
  the positioning stage harder than most: a competitive scan of the category, one named reader, and one
  promise that a P&L-minded audience can repeat back in a sentence. The manuscript is built to be quoted in
  a boardroom or a keynote, not just read cover to cover, so chapters are structured around frameworks and
  case examples a reader can put to work the same week.

faqs:
  - question: "How is a business book different from a memoir or a how-to guide?"
    answer: >-
      A business book sells you as the authority first and teaches second. Every chapter still needs to
      deliver real value, but the underlying job of the book is to make the case for why your firm, your
      framework, or your consulting practice is the one to hire.
  - question: "Do I need an original framework to write one?"
    answer: >-
      It helps, but most experts already have one. It usually lives in your slides and your sales calls
      without a name yet. Positioning work in the Engineer stage is often where that framework finally gets
      named and structured.
  - question: "Will this read like a 200-page sales pitch?"
    answer: >-
      No, and readers can smell that from the first page. The book teaches real material generously. The
      positioning is what happens around the teaching: which reader, which promise, which next step, not
      turning every chapter into an ad.

    answer: >-

      partnership publishing and a traditional deal that can tie up your material.
