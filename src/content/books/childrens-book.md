answerBlock: >-
  A children's book gets written short, then built with an illustrator, not drawn by you. The process
  starts by positioning the book for one age range and one specific reading moment, drafts a tight text
  built for that word count, then brings in illustration and design as a collaborative stage of production,
  not an afterthought. Editing, publishing, and launch follow, and you keep 100% of your rights and
  royalties.
whoWritesThis: >-
  Grandparents writing for grandchildren, experts translating their message for a younger audience, and
  storytellers with one specific idea for one specific child or classroom write these. You don't need to be
  able to draw. You need one clear idea that works read aloud.
painPoints:
  - objection: "I have an idea, but I can't draw at all. Doesn't that stop me?"
    reframe: >-
      No, and this is the most common worry we hear about this book type. Illustration is a collaboration
      stage, not something you're expected to do yourself. An illustrator is matched to your story, and you
      review and approve the art at every step.
  - objection: "I don't know how short a children's book actually needs to be."
    reframe: >-
      Shorter than most first drafts, and that's normal to get wrong at first. Picture books run a tight
      page and word count built for reading aloud in one sitting; positioning sets that target early so the
      draft isn't rewritten from scratch later.
  - objection: "What if kids find it boring even if the parents like the message?"
    reframe: >-
      A children's book has to work for the child in the room, not just the adult buying it. Read-aloud
      pacing, repetition, and a genuine kid's-eye moment of fun get built into the structure, not treated as
      optional decoration.
  - objection: "Who owns the illustrations once the book is done?"
    reframe: >-
      This gets settled in writing before any art is commissioned, so there's no ambiguity later. You keep
      100% of your rights to the book, and illustration rights and usage are laid out clearly as part of
      that agreement.
processDifferences: >-
  A children's book adds an illustration and collaboration stage that a standard nonfiction extraction
  never touches, and it runs on a page-count and word-count structure closer to 32 to 40 pages than
  chapters. Positioning targets one specific age range and reading moment instead of one adult reader and
  promise. Read-aloud pacing and repetition get tested during drafting, since the final judge of the book is
  a child listening, not an adult skimming.
